# Methodology & honest assumptions

This project is a *learning-grade* toolkit. Every simplification is listed here so you can argue about it in an interview — that is the point.

## 1. Proxy aircraft instead of C919 data

COMAC does not publish open performance data for the C919. We therefore model an **A320neo-class proxy** (~170 seats, LEAP-1-class engines, narrow-body). Parameters in `ProxyAircraft` are approximate values assembled from public aircraft specifications:

| Parameter | Value used | Status |
|---|---|---|
| MTOW | 79 000 kg | public spec, approximate |
| OEW | 44 300 kg | approximate |
| Max fuel | 19 000 kg | approximate tank capacity |
| Max structural payload | 18 500 kg | approximate |
| Cruise TAS | 833 km/h (≈ M0.78 @ FL350) | approximate |
| Cruise fuel flow | 2 300 kg/h | order-of-magnitude, constant |
| Climb+descent allowance | 0.2 h | fixed |

Integrating the research-grade [OpenAP](https://github.com/junzis/openap) A320neo model as an optional backend is planned for v0.2; the proxy stays as the dependency-free default.

## 2. Great-circle geometry

Distances use the haversine formula on a sphere (mean radius 6 371.0088 km). Versus true geodesics on the WGS-84 ellipsoid the error is at most ~0.5 % for these ranges. Real airline routes are also not great circles (airways, SIDs/STARs, weather avoidance) — expect a further few percent.

## 3. Constant cruise fuel flow

Fuel flow is treated as constant regardless of weight, altitude, temperature and step climbs. In reality fuel flow scales with weight (a heavy early-cruise aircraft burns noticeably more), so our trip fuel is biased low on long stages and high on short ones. This is the single largest modelling error; it is accepted because the project's goal is transparent, reproducible reasoning rather than accurate numbers.

## 4. Payload–range envelope

The envelope is generated by splitting `MTOW − OEW` between payload and fuel and capping fuel at tank capacity:

1. **MTOW-limited segment** (high payload): fuel = MTOW − OEW − payload → range rises as payload decreases;
2. **Fuel-capacity segment** (low payload): fuel = max fuel → constant maximum range.

This reproduces the classic textbook shape without pretending to certify-grad precision.

## 5. Fuel policy (simplified CCAR-121 / ICAO Annex 6 frame)

`block = taxi + trip + contingency(5 % of trip) + alternate(leg fuel + approach allowance) + final reserve(30 min hold at cruise flow)`.

- The 30-min-hold final reserve follows the ICAO turbine convention; CCAR-121 for domestic operations instead cites 45 min at normal cruise consumption. `FuelPolicy.final_reserve_min` lets you switch; neither choice is authoritative here.
- No contingency for weather re-routing, no extra-fuel policies, no tankering, no ETOPS logic.

## 6. Takeoff field length: heuristic only

`takeoff_field_length_m` applies linear factors to a 2 000 m sea-level-ISA base: +7 % per 1 000 ft pressure altitude, +0.6 % per °C above ISA, −1 % per m/s headwind. These are order-of-magnitude heuristics for teaching how hot-and-high airports eat payload — **not** performance engineering, and landing field length is not modelled yet.

## 7. What this project is NOT

- Not a flight-planning or dispatch tool;
- Not affiliated with or endorsed by COMAC;
- Not based on any non-public data.
